# Explainer-story reels: 19 Oct 2026 (current version)

Style: follow one ordinary character, then slow down and explain what is really happening step by step ("Here's what really happens"), then a twist and the DM ask. Our own scripts; only the storytelling format is borrowed. Characters are fictional and never presented as Mez Media clients. Ad mechanics are real: Meta's auction rewards ads people engage with (estimated action rates and ad quality), Boost optimises for engagement/clicks rather than purchases, and the Meta Pixel powers retargeting.

## Reel 1: Your ad's first second (Sarah on the train)

**Visual plan:** Frame 1: a giant stopwatch at 0.00 over a B&W train carriage. Thumb flicking a feed at speed; slow-motion when the ad appears; a 'thought bubble' IS THIS FOR ME?; logo ad gets stamped ADVERT and flies off; a 'Meta' control-room view of skip counters ticking and a price tag rising; REWIND tape effect; cracked-heel close-up; thumb stops; price tag falling.

```
This is your ad's first second.
Meet Sarah. 7:42 a.m., on the train, scrolling.
Her thumb is flying.
Then your ad appears.
Her brain asks one question.
"Is this for me?"
Your ad opens on your logo.
Her brain sees a logo and files it under "advert."
Her thumb doesn't even slow down.
Gone.
But here's the part nobody tells you.
Meta was watching.
It saw Sarah skip. Then the next person. And the next.
So Meta decides people don't like your ad.
And it charges you more to show it to fewer people.
Now, rewind.
Same train. Same Sarah.
This time, your ad opens on a problem she has.
Cracked heels. On screen. Second one.
Her thumb stops.
She watches. She taps.
Meta notices that too.
So it shows your ad to more people like Sarah, for less.
Same product. Same budget. Different first second.
DM me "teardown" and I'll fix your first second.
```

## Reel 2: Paying for likes (Tom and the boost button)

**Visual plan:** Frame 1: a £3,000 receipt printing next to a giant heart icon. Tom's hoodie posts; the BOOST button pressed in slow motion; Meta drawn as a radar finding 'likers' (heart-shaped dots); comment bubbles 'fire 🔥'; empty till; twist: the radar switches to shopping-bag dots; order notifications.

```
This man paid 3,000 pounds for likes.
Meet Tom. He sells hoodies online.
Every Friday, he picks his best post and presses one button.
Boost.
Here's what really happens.
Meta asks itself, "What does Tom want?"
And the boost button answers: likes, comments, clicks.
So Meta goes hunting for people who like everything.
They like Tom's post. They comment "fire."
And they never, ever buy.
Tom sees 2,000 likes and thinks it's working.
His bank account says otherwise.
Now, here's the twist.
Meta can find buyers just as easily.
Tom just never asked it to.
So he switches to Ads Manager. Sales goal. Website connected.
Now Meta asks a different question.
"Who actually buys hoodies after seeing an ad?"
Fewer likes. More orders.
Meta finds whoever you ask for.
DM me "teardown" before you pay for likes again.
```

## Reel 3: Why ads follow you (Aisha and the pixel)

**Visual plan:** Frame 1: a pair of trainers walking behind a girl on a dark street. Laptop at night, tab closing; a tiny glowing pixel writing a note; Mon/Tue/Wed/Thu calendar flips, each with a different ad; checkout; twist: a second shop down the road with its lights on but nobody inside.

```
Why do those trainers keep following you?
Meet Aisha. Monday night, she looks at a pair of trainers online.
She doesn't buy. She closes the tab.
But here's what she didn't see.
The moment the page loaded, a tiny piece of code made a note.
It's called a pixel.
"Aisha looked at these trainers. Size 6."
Tuesday morning, she opens Instagram.
There they are. The same trainers.
Wednesday, a different ad. A customer review.
"Most comfortable shoes I've ever owned."
Thursday, one more.
"Free delivery ends tonight."
Aisha buys.
She thinks she decided on Thursday.
The brand planned it on Monday.
Now here's the twist.
Down the road there's another shop. Better trainers. Cheaper too.
Aisha looked at those first.
But that shop never installed a pixel.
So it never saw her again.
DM me "teardown". I'll check who's following your customers.
```
